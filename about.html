The developer behind the systems.

I am a senior full-stack developer in Dhaka, Bangladesh. For the past several years I have done one thing in many forms: build the software a business actually runs on — and stay responsible for it after launch.

I build large-scale, production systems for e-commerce, agriculture, hospitality and retail businesses. My work goes beyond websites. I design and build the complete operational system behind a business — the customer-facing storefront, the internal ERP and administration, the point-of-sale software used at the counter, and the mobile applications used in the field — and I stay responsible for it from architecture and database design through deployment, server administration and long-term maintenance.

That range is deliberate. When one person holds the whole chain — storefront, backend, counter, server — the pieces fit: the price a customer sees is the price the cashier charges is the price in the owner's report. Most of the systems on this site are still mine to run today, which keeps me honest about the difference between software that demos well and software that survives a Friday rush, a campaign spike, or a dropped internet connection.

Alongside business systems, I work hands-on with AI: QLoRA fine-tuning of Qwen language models for Bangla and Banglish, autonomous agent platforms with persistent memory, and multi-model research workflows. My own platform, Bahar Autonomous Evolution AI, is where that research lives — run with the same discipline: nothing is adopted without evaluation against a baseline.

I am interested in challenging, large-scale problems where software has to run a real business every day. If that is what you are building, tell me about it.

How a project runs

Four stages, no surprises. You always know what is built, what it costs, and what comes next.

1

Understand

We go through your business process — what you sell, who uses the system, which reports matter.

2

Design

You approve the structure and screens before heavy building starts. Changes are cheap here, expensive later.

3

Build in stages

You review working software at each stage, not a big reveal at the end. Feedback goes in while it still helps.

4

Launch & handover

Deployment, training and documentation. The system and its source are yours — no lock-in to me.
