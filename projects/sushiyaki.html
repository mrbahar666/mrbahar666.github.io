Overview

Sushi Yaki is a Japanese restaurant brand in Uttara, Dhaka. A restaurant's technology has to disappear into the service: guests order without friction, the counter bills without delays, and the owners see one clear picture of the business at the end of the day. My work for Sushi Yaki, as IT Project Manager, covers that whole chain.

The public side is sushiyakibd.com — the restaurant website with online ordering through WooCommerce, so the menu customers browse is the menu they can order from. Behind the website sits a complete ERP ecosystem: the web ERP for operations, an offline-capable Windows POS at the counter that keeps billing even when the internet drops, and QR table ordering for dine-in guests.

The pieces are integrated rather than merely coexisting: the same products and prices feed the website, the ERP and the counter, and orders from every channel land in one operational view. That integration is what lets a multi-format restaurant — dine-in, delivery and takeaway — run on one set of numbers instead of three reconciliations.

The website also presents the brand's standing online offer up front — visitors learn about it before checkout, not after — because an offer nobody sees until the last step does not sell anything. My responsibility continues past launch: platform updates, ordering-flow checks and the server side all sit with me, so the restaurant's busiest hour is never the moment something is tried for the first time. It is unglamorous work, and it is the work that keeps a restaurant's technology invisible.
