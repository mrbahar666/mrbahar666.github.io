Overview

Fish farming has its own rhythm: ponds are stocked, fed and harvested on cycles that stretch across months, and a farmer managing several ponds needs to know — for each pond — what went in, what it cost, and what came out. Fish Manager is the aquaculture companion to Poultry Manager, built for exactly that record-keeping.

The Android application tracks ponds individually: stocking and production records, feed tracking across the cycle, and the reporting a farmer needs to judge whether a pond made money. Like its poultry counterpart, it is built natively with Kotlin, for farmers working on ordinary phones in ordinary connectivity — the record has to be quick to enter at the pond side, or it will not be entered at all.

Together, the two applications reflect a consistent approach I take to agricultural software: model the real unit of work — the batch, the pond — keep daily entry nearly effortless, and make the economics visible early enough to change a decision. Both applications came out of my work with Manu Farms, where the same team runs the farms, the platform and the applications, so every feature answers to a real operational need.

Pond cycles punish late information more than most: by harvest, the feed decisions that decided the profit were made months earlier. Fish Manager's reporting is built to surface the economics while the cycle is still running — feed in, growth on track, expected return — so a farmer can correct course on the current pond, not just learn from the last one.

What it includes

  • Per-pond records — each pond tracked as its own unit
  • Stocking & production tracking across the cycle
  • Feed tracking from stocking to harvest
  • Cycle economics reporting — whether the pond made money
