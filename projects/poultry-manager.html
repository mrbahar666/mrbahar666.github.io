Overview

Poultry farming runs on thin margins and daily decisions: how much feed went out, which flock received medicine, what a batch truly cost by the time it sells. On paper, those answers arrive weeks late. Poultry Manager is the Android application I worked on at Manu Farms to put them in the farmer's hand the same day.

The app covers the day-to-day records of a poultry farm — flock records across batches, feed tracking, medicine schedules and cost reporting — so a farmer can see the state of the farm and the economics of each batch without reconstructing them from notebooks. It is built natively for Android with Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, designed for the devices and connectivity farmers actually have.

The application's reach goes beyond record-keeping. Its public site at manufarms.com documents the app's features and its IoT devices, and records an MoU signing with BRAC Bank that made SME loans accessible to farmers using the app — a sign of the app's role as infrastructure for the farms on it, not just a diary. My work spanned the application and the platform around it: the product, its data model, and the operations that keep it useful season after season.

The data model follows the farm's own unit of work — the batch. Feed, medicine and costs attach to a batch from placement to sale, so the final report answers the only question that matters: what did this batch earn? Everything else in the app exists to make entering that data take seconds, at the shed, on the phone already in the farmer's pocket.

What it includes

  • Flock & batch records across the production cycle
  • Feed tracking per flock and batch
  • Medicine schedules kept with the flock record
  • Cost & batch economics reporting — what a batch truly cost
  • IoT device support presented alongside the app
  • Built for real conditions — ordinary phones, ordinary connectivity
